Fleet operations management revamped.

Rebuilding a long-ignored back-end feature into a map-first tool fleet managers and street operators actually wanted to use — and watching adoption climb from "rarely" to 72%.

Wunder's back-end fleet management — assigning repairs, refuelling, pickups to street operators — had existed for years but was rarely used. Two reasons: it wasn't easily accessible from where work actually happened, and the assignment / update / overview flow wasn't user-friendly.

End-to-end product design. Audited the old system, interviewed clients, mapped journeys for two personas (Fleet Manager and Street Operator), ideated with how-might-we framing, prototyped, tested, shipped, and followed through Design QA.

Figma for design and prototypes. Miro for Who/What/Why/Where, journey mapping, and how-might-we workshops. Our internal Design System for fast iteration. Productboard for opportunity sizing and prioritisation.

04 — What Shipped Four shifts, one connected experience
Pillar 01

Tasks on the Operations Map.

Task management moved from a buried back-end screen to the side panel of the vehicle it concerns. Create, assign and update tasks without ever leaving the map.

Pillar 02

Hardware actions & IoT info.

Lock, unlock, honk, open the tailbox, see battery and last-known location — every hardware action and IoT data point sits next to the relevant task.

Pillar 03

Inline notes.

Multiple notes per vehicle, timestamped and authored, replacing the spreadsheets and WhatsApp messages clients were using to track context outside the system.

Pillar 04

Mobile-friendly for the street.

A dedicated mobile-web surface for street operators — task list, new-task form, hardware actions, notes — so the people doing the work could finally work from their phones.

05 — Process Three phases · click any image to expand
Phase 01
phase-research.jpgWho / What / Why / Where — the questions board

Research

Audited the old system end-to-end and applied a Who · What · Why · Where investigation to surface user, task, purpose and context. Followed with interviews — adoption rate, usage patterns, workarounds clients had built in Excel, Trello and WhatsApp.

Phase 02
phase-personas.jpgCustomer Journey Map — Street Operator

Map the personas

Built journey maps for the two primary personas — the Fleet Manager overseeing the operation from a desk, and the Street Operator doing the work on the road. Same data, very different needs and channels.

Phase 03
phase-ideate.jpgHow Might We — the question board

Ideate & hypothesise

Reframed insights as how-might-we questions and ran them against possible solutions. Settled the hypothesis: move task management to the map, make it mobile-friendly, embed hardware actions and notes inline.

07 — Lessons Learned Four takeaways from rescuing a long-ignored feature
Lesson 01

Accessibility is the first feature.

The old task management was good enough — it just lived two clicks away from where the work happened. Moving it next to the vehicle on the operations map did more for adoption than any new functionality could have.

Lesson 02

One screen, two personas.

The fleet manager and street operator wanted to see the same data, just framed differently. Journey-mapping both surfaced the shared core — and let us avoid building two separate products.

Lesson 03

Tight scripts, sharp learnings.

User testing on exactly three tasks — create a task from the map, fire a hardware action, write a note — surfaced precisely the friction we needed to act on. A broader script would have collected noise.

Lesson 04

If it's not mobile, it doesn't exist.

Street operators were ignoring the feature partly because they were on their phones, not at desks. Shipping a mobile-web surface unlocked the persona who was supposed to be the heaviest user all along.

— 08 · Reflection
The old task management lived two clicks from where the work happened. The new one lives next to the vehicle on the map. Same data, same flows — moved exactly one screen. That's all it took for adoption to climb from "rarely" to seventy-two percent.
