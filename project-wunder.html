Mobility Subscription Plans — enabling user engagement.

Adding a white-label subscription model to a mobility platform — letting one feature scale across operators offering cars, bikes and kick-scooters, without becoming an agency for any of them.

Multiple clients (mobility operators offering cars, bikes and kick-scooters) had flagged the same request: subscription plans. They saw the business model as a way to boost profitability — but each one wanted it slightly differently.

End-to-end product design on a cross-functional mobile team: discovery sessions, competitor research, prototyping, user testing, validation and Design QA. Related reading: my article on white-label design.

Figma for design and prototypes, our internal Design System for fast iteration, Productboard for use-case mapping and prioritization, moderated user testing with a focused three-task script.

03 — What Shipped (MVP) Four configurable capabilities, one shared surface
Feature 01

Configurable plan list.

End-users see a list of subscriptions configured by each operator — same surface, different content. Operators set the plans up in the backend; end-users see them in their app.

Feature 02

Credits & discounts.

Each plan can include monthly riding credits and ride discounts as perks. The plan card shows both clearly, so end-users understand value at a glance.

Feature 03

Refill or stack.

Operators choose whether credits refill monthly (unused credits expire) or stack (carry-over). One toggle in the backend, two distinct behaviours in the app.

Feature 04

Operator-owned copy.

Every plan title, short description and long description is configurable per operator. The frontend hides empty fields automatically so the layout never breaks.

04 — Process Three phases · click any image to expand
Phase 01
phase-discover.jpgCompetitor analysis — car / bike / kick-scooter

Discover

Studied how MILES, Donkey Republic, Voi, Lime, Tier, Bird and others structured their subscription offers. Key insight: across vehicle types, plan benefits look almost identical — credits, discounts, fee waivers — even when the brands feel worlds apart.

Phase 02
phase-define.jpgProductboard flow — use cases, frontend & backend

Define

Sat with clients to validate assumptions, then paired with the PO and engineers to scope an MVP that fit the technical constraints. Postponed nice-to-haves (free unlocks, fee discounts) so the first version could ship across all operators without becoming a per-client agency build.

Phase 03
phase-test.jpgDesign spec — annotated flow board

Design & Test

Prototyped fast on top of our Design System and ran moderated tests with a tight three-task script: subscribe, replace, cancel. Testing surfaced minor copy and confirmation refinements; the core flow held up. Followed development through Design QA.

06 — Lessons Learned Four takeaways from shipping a white-label feature
Lesson 01

Design for the common, not the custom.

In a white-label product, the trap is building for one excited client. The real job is finding the pattern that ten clients can all configure — and saying no to the rest, kindly but firmly.

Lesson 02

A Design System is a multiplier.

Going from "let's prototype" to "ready for testing" took days, not weeks. The system did the heavy lifting so I could spend time on the parts that were actually new — credit logic, plan switching, edge states.

Lesson 03

Three tasks, three insights.

Tightly scoped testing — subscribe, replace, cancel — surfaced exactly the kind of friction we needed to fix. A broader script would have collected noise we couldn't act on inside the MVP scope.

Lesson 04

Pair with PO & engineers early.

Scoping the MVP with engineering already in the room meant we cut "free unlocks" and "fee discounts" before they hit Figma. Less rework, less heartbreak, faster path to ship.

— 07 · Reflection
When you build white-label, your most important user isn't the end-user — it's the operator who will configure the feature for thousands of end-users. Every choice has to make sense at both layers, or it makes sense at neither.
