The MVP was live but had rough edges. The aggregated total balance worried us in research (was it confusing?) — interviews said it wasn't, but it could be clearer. Transaction navigation was clunky. And we still had no real menu — fine for the MVP, untenable as soon as we added cards controls.

Continued as the lead product designer on the web team. Drove discovery and user testing for cards controls. Designed Account overview 2.0, transaction details, the first navigation, and the entire cards-control surface. See also the MVP case study.

Figma for design and prototypes. Moderated user interviews to validate the total-balance concern. Moderated user testing on cards controls, which surfaced specific pain points around virtual cards and card-status copy.

03 — What Shipped Four feature areas, one cohesive web product
Pillar 01

Account overview 2.0.

Total balance now shows as a donut chart, split by currency and pot. Tabs split Personal, Business and Other accounts. Same data — clearer surface.

Pillar 02

Transaction details.

Modal opens on the transaction list with prev/next arrows on either side. Skim through transactions without leaving the page, no back-button gymnastics.

Pillar 03

Cards controls.

Order or reorder physical cards, create virtual cards, view PIN/CVC and expiry, lock and unlock, cancel or report stolen — all from the browser.

Pillar 04

A real navigation.

Cards controls pushed us past the menu-less MVP. Built a quick-access dropdown for cards alongside the Accounts nav — designed to scale as the product grows.

Phase 01

Listen

Interviewed customers about the aggregated total balance. Result: most got it without issue. So we kept the concept and focused on making it more legible rather than removing it.

Phase 02

Design

Visualised the total balance as a donut. Reframed transactions as a modal with prev/next arrows. Designed the full cards-control surface and the first dropdown navigation — staged so each could ship on its own.

Phase 03
phase-test.jpgUser testing — cards controls

Test

Ran moderated testing on the cards-control flow. Surfaced two clear pain points: not enough info about virtual cards, and confusing copy around card status. Both were fixed before ship.

06 — Lessons Learned Four takeaways from the v2 cycle
Lesson 01

Validate before refactoring.

We almost rebuilt total balance. Interviews showed most users got it — so we polished instead. Asking five users a single sharp question saves weeks.

Lesson 02

Features force systems.

We didn't need a real navigation until cards controls came along — then suddenly we did. New features expose architectural gaps that were tolerable yesterday.

Lesson 03

Modals are journeys.

The single biggest win in transactions was adding prev/next arrows to the detail modal. Users come to check one transaction and end up checking five.

Lesson 04

Test the vocabulary.

Cards-control testing failed on copy, not interaction. "Lock" vs "Cancel" vs "Report" needed different framing — and "virtual card" needed an explainer right next to it.

— 07 · Reflection
An MVP earns the right to grow. The v2 cycle wasn't about adding features — it was about turning a useful first version into a product customers chose, not just the one their phone defaulted to.
